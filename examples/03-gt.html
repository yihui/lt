Migrating from gt to lt

  • 1 The most common calls
  • 2 What to watch for

This is the short version of the guide for users coming from gt. We deeply appreciate gt’s pioneering work: lt owes much of its design vocabulary — headers, spanners, row groups, footnotes — to the ground gt broke. lt targets HTML output only, and in exchange keeps its dependencies and its runtime tiny: gt pulls in ~54 recursive dependencies, whereas lt depends only on xfun.

A happy coincidence fits the spirit of this trade-off: in the comparison operators gt is greater than and lt is less than — lt does deliberately less.

1 The most common calls

gt lt
gt(x) lt(x)
gt(x, groupname_col = "col") lt(x) |> lt_group(~ col, sep = TRUE)
tab_header(title=, subtitle=) lt_header(title=, subtitle=)
tab_spanner(label=, columns=) lt_spanner(label=, columns=)
cols_label(a = "A") lt_label(a = "A")
tab_footnote(footnote, cells_body(col, rows)) lt_footnote(text, "body", columns, rows)
tab_source_note(source_note=) lt_note(text)
fmt_number(columns=, decimals=) lt_format(columns, decimals=)
sub_missing() / sub_zero() lt_sub(missing=, zero=)
opt_interactive() lt_interactive()
gtsave(file=) lt_export(file=)

2 What to watch for

  • Columns are selected by name or position (or a one-sided formula), not tidy-select helpers.
  • Interactivity is one function, lt_interactive(), not a separate step.
  • Footnotes (lt_footnote()) and source notes (lt_note()) are two functions.
  • Row groups render as rowspan cells or separator rows (sep =).
  • Extend lt() with S3 methods rather than a new generic.
  • Styling rarely needs CSS: try lt_style() first.

The complete guide in the book has the full function mapping, the differences in detail, and a runnable example. If you maintain a package that ships tables, see Building on lt.
